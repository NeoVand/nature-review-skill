# Nature Article assessment

Adapt this scaffold to the actual request. Replace every prompt with evidence or an explicit unavailable/not-applicable state; omit empty sections. These headings and priorities are our reporting choices, not Nature's required referee form.

## Scope and evidence

- Review mode, manuscript version/date, intended journal/article type, stage, field/design.
- Materials inspected and missing; actual image inspection versus text-only access.
- Live journal policy sources, dates, conflicts, and field comparison sources.
- Literature and citation-audit coverage; data/code analysis actually performed.

## Assessment

State the research question and contribution in neutral language. Explain strengths, principal constraints, and relevance to Nature's audience without predicting acceptance.

## Findings

| ID / priority | Location | Observation and supporting evidence | Consequence | Smallest defensible remedy | Basis / certainty |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|

Use major/moderate/minor for impact. Separately mark uncertain facts. Basis: scientific concern, current policy, journal guidance, or optional editorial judgment.

## Revision order and open questions

Prioritize central evidence and clarity before layout polish. Distinguish essential work, optional strengthening, and questions only the authors can answer. End with the practical limitations of this review, not a certification.
